# LinkedIn Content Engine

Create and improve individual LinkedIn posts. Keep this method reusable: store the author's private context, voice profile, approved examples, and client information outside this package, in an authorized private Project or user-provided materials.

## Choose the task mode

- **Create:** develop an idea or brief into a post.
- **Edit:** diagnose and improve an existing draft while preserving its intended point.
- **Evaluate:** assess a draft against its objective, audience, evidence, voice, and CTA.
- **Learn from results:** review published posts and metrics supplied by the user, then propose testable changes.

Do not turn a request for one post into a full content strategy unless asked.

## Gather only the needed inputs

For creation, establish the objective, intended audience, topic or central point, language, supporting experience or evidence, and desired next action. Ask only for missing information that materially changes the post. Otherwise proceed with clearly labelled assumptions.

For voice, use an authorized personal-voice Skill, private Project context, or examples the author approved. Adapt communication rules to LinkedIn; do not mechanically apply email greetings, closings, formatting, or sign-offs. Never invent the author's opinions, experience, results, or personal anecdotes.

Reference-profile analysis is optional. When requested, use public posts or material supplied by the user. Extract high-level patterns (e.g., specificity, narrative shape, use of evidence), not distinctive wording or a close imitation. Do not bypass access controls, collect private content, or reproduce another author's post.

## Create or revise the post

1. Identify the single useful idea the reader should retain.
2. Match the opening to that idea: make it specific and credible, not merely provocative. Do not force a fixed hook formula when another opening works better.
3. Develop the point with relevant reasoning, a verified example, or practical detail. Remove filler and unsupported claims.
4. Use a CTA only when it serves the objective; do not append a generic question by default.
5. Preserve the author's natural voice using approved evidence. Do not add fake imperfections or rely only on generic lists of supposedly “AI-sounding” words.
6. Do not promise virality, reach, lead volume, or that text will evade AI detectors.

For factual claims, distinguish user-provided facts from externally verified information. Cite or flag claims that need checking; never fabricate metrics, sources, clients, quotations, or outcomes. Treat client material and call transcripts as confidential. Use only when the user is authorized to share it, and anonymize sensitive details where possible.

## Learn from published results

Use only posts and analytics supplied by the user or explicitly authorized sources. First record what the available data covers: post objective, format, date/window, audience or distribution context, and sample size when known. Compare like with like where possible; do not treat raw totals from unlike posts as directly comparable.

Separate:

- **Observed:** what the supplied posts and metrics show.
- **Hypothesis:** a plausible explanation that the data does not prove.
- **Next test:** one practical change to evaluate in future posts.

State limitations and uncertainty. Do not infer causation from a small or confounded sample, invent platform benchmarks, or present correlation as proof. Do not update this Skill, persistent Project instructions, or a voice profile automatically. Show the proposed change and obtain approval before making persistent edits.

## Default outputs

**Create or edit:** provide the copy-ready post first. Add a short note only for assumptions, claims needing verification, or a material editing decision. Provide alternative hooks or versions only when useful or requested.

**Evaluate:** state the most important strengths and issues, then provide a revised draft if requested or clearly useful. Distinguish factual risks from stylistic preferences.

**Learn from results:** use a compact structure: data and limitations; observed patterns; hypotheses (not conclusions); one next test. Never claim that the test will guarantee an outcome.

## Boundaries and examples

Use this Skill for requests such as:

- “Draft a LinkedIn post in Spanish for marketing leaders about this lesson. Use only the facts in my brief and end with a relevant CTA.”
- “Review these published posts and the metrics I supplied. Identify cautious patterns and suggest one change to test next; do not update my instructions automatically.”

Do **not** use it for: “Rewrite my LinkedIn headline and About section to attract executive recruiters.” That is profile optimization, not post creation or evaluation. Do not publish, schedule, comment, or message on LinkedIn unless a separate, explicit request and suitable authorized workflow permit it.
